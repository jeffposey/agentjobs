# AgentJobs task corpus audit

Audit date: 2026-08-13

Two dated reports live in this file. This one is the 2026-08-13 structural audit;
[the record-quality baseline](#record-quality-baseline) below is 2026-08-27 and is
regenerated by a command rather than counted by hand.

This report records the repository-wide task audit performed by
[task 103](https://github.com/jeffposey/agentjobs/blob/main/tasks/agentjobs/task-103-agentjobs-task-corpus-audit.yaml). The audit
started with 76 YAML records under `tasks/agentjobs/`, inspected every record, and
created three explicit follow-ups. The review snapshot therefore contains 79 records:
51 closed, 22 ready, five draft, and task 103 active.

## Checks performed

- Loaded every YAML record through the schema-v2 model rather than sampling files.
- Accounted for lifecycle, ball, outcome, acceptance, deliverable, branch, parent,
  dependency, context-path, and log state.
- Checked every `parent` and dependency target against the complete id set; none
  dangle.
- Checked every concrete repository-relative `spec.context` path; none are missing.
- Reviewed all open summaries, descriptions, acceptance criteria, deliverables, and
  context pointers for pre-React UI instructions.
- Reviewed closed records with pending acceptance for later evidence or an explicit
  reason the criterion remains pending.
- Reviewed the exceptional open task with a merged branch and confirmed that task 075
  intentionally shipped its documentation layer while retaining dispatch work.
- Ran the task-corpus tests and the complete repository gate after reconciliation.

# Record-quality baseline

Measured 2026-08-27 by [task 306](https://github.com/jeffposey/agentjobs/blob/main/tasks/agentjobs/task-306.yaml),
from finding F-14 of the 2026-08-21 context audit
(`audits/2026-08-21/01-context-architecture.md` §6). The audit above checked the corpus
for structure and currency. This section measures something else — how far the records
have drifted from the four properties ALLAGENTS' Resumption Contract asks of them — and
it exists as a **baseline**: F-14 had to derive its figures with a throwaway script
because no earlier audit had left any behind, and this section is so the next one does
not have to.

**Regenerate it with one command.** Nothing here is hand-counted:

```bash
poetry run python scripts/corpus_stats.py
poetry run python scripts/corpus_stats.py --tasks-dir <path>   # somebody else's corpus
```

The script reads a directory of task files, not the store. On a project that has been
cut over to SQLite — this one, since 2026-09-07 — the default directory is the frozen
pre-cutover copy, so export first and point `--tasks-dir` at the export:

```bash
poetry run agentjobs storage export <somewhere outside the repo> --project agentjobs
```

It is deliberately not in `scripts/check.py`. Nothing it prints can fail, and a gate
stage that cannot fail is one people stop reading.

## 2026-08-27: 315 records, 2,549 log entries

### Questions and answers reaching the record

**42 `question` and `answer` entries in 2,549 — 1.6 per 100 — spread across 24 of 315
records.** This is the figure to compare against next time.

| Entry type | Count | Per 100 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `transition` | 653 | 25.6 |
| `note` | 524 | 20.6 |
| `progress` | 419 | 16.4 |
| `handoff` | 363 | 14.2 |
| `decision` | 231 | 9.1 |
| `dispatch_result` | 111 | 4.4 |
| `dispatch` | 107 | 4.2 |
| `queue_move` | 73 | 2.9 |
| **`question`** | **31** | **1.2** |
| `instruction` | 26 | 1.0 |
| **`answer`** | **11** | **0.4** |

The comparison is the finding, not the absolute number: 31 questions against 419
progress entries and 231 decisions says that sessions are recording what they did and
what they chose, and almost never what they were unsure about. ALLAGENTS is explicit
that a question asked in chat is not queryable and does not survive the session, so
either the work has been almost free of uncertainty or the uncertainty is going
somewhere it cannot be read. F-14 measured 15 questions and 7 answers on 2026-08-21;
six days later that is 31 and 11, which is movement in the right direction from a very
low base and not yet a trend.

### Summary length, against "one or two sentences"

| Era | Records | Median words | Longest | Over 40 words |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 001–030 | 24 | 53 | 80 | 21 (88%) |
| 031–105 | 77 | 35 | 116 | 30 (39%) |
| 106–185 | 81 | 43 | 69 | 47 (58%) |
| 186– | 133 | 60 | 126 | 111 (83%) |
| **all** | **315** | **49** | **126** | **209 (66%)** |

The medians for 031–105 and 106–185 reproduce F-14's exactly, which is the check that
this script measures what the audit measured. The newest era has gone on drifting: 54
words when the audit sampled it at task-243, 60 across the 133 records now in it.

**The 001–030 row is not the oldest era**, despite its ids. Finding F-15 established
that the corpus carries two id series and that 001–024 were created in August 2026,
after task-105 changed id allocation; its median of 53 words is a modern record wearing
an old number. Folding those into 031–105 raises that era's median from 35 to 42 and
destroys the comparison the table exists to make, so they get a row of their own.

### What the write-path warnings would say about existing records

`agentjobs.record_check` raises two warnings — `long_summary` and
`default_ball_prompt` — at the moment a write could have caused one, and never in
`agentjobs validate`. Swept over the whole corpus instead, they would fire on:

| Warning | Records | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `long_summary` | 209 | 66% |
| `default_ball_prompt` | 1 | 0% |

**That first number is the reason the warnings are not in the corpus sweep**, and it is
recorded here so the decision stays checkable rather than being taken on trust. Two
records in three would light up on the first `validate` run in CI, in front of readers
who wrote none of them; the finding on task-306 is that this trains a reader to skim
past the `queue-duplicate` and `dependency-cycle` lines sitting in the same block.
Keeping the number visible to an auditor, and out of a committer's way, is the whole of
the arrangement.
